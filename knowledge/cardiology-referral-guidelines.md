# 🫀 Cardiology Specialist Referral Clinical SOP & Guidelines

## 1. Acute Indications for Specialist Cardiology Referral
An urgent or emergency Cardiology consultation referral is indicated when ANY of the following criteria are met during the current visit:

1. **Acute Chest Pain Protocol**:
   - Patient presents with acute substernal chest pain, pressure, tightness, or angina-equivalent symptoms (e.g. shortness of breath with jaw/arm radiation).
   - Any elevated cardiac biomarker: Serum Cardiac Troponin I **> 0.04 ng/mL** or Troponin T **> 0.01 ng/mL** requires STAT Cardiology evaluation.

2. **ECG Abnormalities**:
   - Recorded 12-lead ECG demonstrating ST-segment elevation/depression, new T-wave inversions, new Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB), or acute ischemic changes.
   - Malignant or hemodynamically unstable arrhythmias (e.g. sustained Ventricular Tachycardia, Atrial Fibrillation with rapid ventricular response HR > 130).

3. **Hemodynamic Instability with Cardiac Symptoms**:
   - NEWS2 Score **≥ 5** accompanied by chest discomfort or dyspnea.
   - Severe Tachycardia (Heart Rate **> 120 bpm**) combined with Hypotension (Systolic BP **< 90 mmHg**).

4. **Acute Heart Failure Exacerbation**:
   - Severe orthopnea, bilateral pulmonary rales, elevated BNP/NT-proBNP, or acute pulmonary edema.

## 2. Mandatory Rules for Specialist Referral
- **Current Visit Fact Constraint**: A Cardiology referral MUST be grounded in objective findings from the **current visit** (current vitals, current labs, current ECG, or acute chief complaint).
- **Historical Condition Exclusions**: A history of past Coronary Artery Disease (CAD), past hypertension, or past cardiac stent installation alone—WITHOUT acute symptoms or abnormal current parameters—does **NOT** justify an urgent cardiology referral.
- **Decision Support Output**: All AI recommendation outputs must generate proposals for human doctor review and include verifiable evidence citations.
