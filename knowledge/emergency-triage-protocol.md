# 🚨 Emergency Department Triage & Escalation SOP

## 1. Emergency Severity Index (ESI) Triage Levels
- **Level 1 (Resuscitation)**: Immediate life-saving intervention required (e.g., Cardiac Arrest, Anaphylactic Shock, Severe Respiratory Distress). Immediate physician bedside attendance required.
- **Level 2 (Emergent)**: High-risk situation, confused/lethargic/disoriented patient, or severe pain/distress. Maximum wait time to physician evaluation: **10 minutes**.
- **Level 3 (Urgent)**: Requires two or more resources (e.g. Labs, X-Ray, IV Fluids). Vital signs must be within non-critical parameters. Maximum wait time: **30 minutes**.

## 2. Critical Lab & Vital Sign Thresholds (Red Alerts)
- **Fasting Blood Glucose Alerts**:
  - **Hyperglycemic Alert**: Blood Glucose **> 250 mg/dL** requires immediate STAT insulin protocol initiation and notification of attending endocrinologist.
  - **Hypoglycemic Alert**: Blood Glucose **< 50 mg/dL** requires immediate IV Dextrose (D50W) administration.
- **Blood Pressure Thresholds**:
  - **Hypertensive Crisis**: Systolic BP **> 180 mmHg** OR Diastolic BP **> 120 mmHg** requires STAT IV antihypertensive administration and continuous cardiac monitoring.

## 3. Attending Physician Escalation Workflow
- **Immediate Escalation Trigger**: Any patient exhibiting two or more abnormal lab indicators (e.g. HbA1c > 10%, Blood Glucose > 250 mg/dL, Creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL) must trigger an immediate automated RED ALERT task for the On-Call Attending Physician.
- **Human-In-The-Loop Approval**: Medication adjustments or high-risk transfers require explicit electronic sign-off from the Attending Physician before patient movement.
