# 🏥 Inpatient Discharge & Follow-up Clinical Policy

## 1. Readiness Criteria for Inpatient Discharge
- **Vital Sign Stability**: Patient vital signs must be documented stable for a minimum of 12 consecutive hours prior to discharge order sign-off (Heart Rate 60-100 bpm, Respiratory Rate 12-20 bpm, Oxygen Saturation > 95% on room air).
- **Physician Attestation**: The attending physician of record must complete and digitally sign the electronic discharge summary.
- **Outpatient Support Verification**: Primary caregiver or home health support must be confirmed prior to transport authorization.

## 2. Mandatory Medication Reconciliation & Allergy Protocols
- **Cross-Referencing Electronic Health Records**: Nurse must reconcile all home medications against hospital discharge prescriptions.
- **Penicillin Allergy Protocol**: If patient has a documented **Penicillin** or Beta-Lactam allergy, all discharge prescriptions must be checked to ensure zero cross-reactive antibiotics (e.g. Amoxicillin, Ampicillin, Piperacillin) are prescribed. Alternative fluoroquinolone or macrolide therapy must be selected.
- **Sulfa Drug Allergy Protocol**: If patient has a documented **Sulfa** allergy, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim) and sulfasalazine must be strictly avoided.

## 3. Mandatory 7-Day Follow-Up Scheduling
- **Lab Test Scheduling**: Patients with abnormal inpatient lab results (e.g. HbA1c > 7.0%, Fasting Blood Glucose > 130 mg/dL, or Serum Creatinine > 1.2 mg/dL) must have a follow-up outpatient blood lab test scheduled within **7 calendar days** of discharge.
- **Task Assignment**: The discharge coordinator must assign a high-priority follow-up task to the Outpatient Clinic Coordinator via the hospital staff task engine using the patient's unique identifier (e.g. P1001).
