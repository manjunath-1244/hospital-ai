# 🧠 Neurology Specialist Referral Clinical SOP & Guidelines

## 1. Acute Indications for Specialist Neurology Referral
An urgent or emergency Neurology consultation referral is indicated when ANY of the following criteria are met during the current visit:

1. **Acute Neurological Deficit (Stroke Protocol / FAST)**:
   - Sudden onset of focal weakness, facial drooping, arm drift, or speech difficulty (aphasia/dysarthria).
   - Acute cranial nerve palsy, hemiparesis, sudden sensory loss, or acute ataxia/gait instability.

2. **Severe or Sudden Headache**:
   - Sudden-onset "thunderclap" headache (reaching peak intensity within seconds/minutes).
   - Severe persistent headache accompanied by focal neurological signs, papilledema, or meningismus.

3. **Altered Consciousness & Seizures**:
   - Level of consciousness altered from baseline (AVPU scale: CONFUSION, VOICE, PAIN, or UNRESPONSIVE).
   - New-onset seizure, status epilepticus, or post-ictal confusion with persistent neurological deficit.

4. **Hypertensive Crisis with Neurological Symptoms**:
   - Systolic BP **> 180 mmHg** OR Diastolic BP **> 120 mmHg** accompanied by acute headache, visual disturbance, or confusion.

## 2. Mandatory Rules for Specialist Referral
- **Current Visit Fact Constraint**: A Neurology referral MUST be supported by documented physical examination findings, vitals, or acute symptoms recorded during the **current visit**.
- **Historical Condition Exclusions**: Past history of tension headache, remote concussion, or chronic migraines without new red-flag symptoms or focal deficits does **NOT** warrant an emergency neurology referral.
- **Decision Support Output**: Referral outputs are clinical recommendations for human doctor confirmation and must cite verified evidence from the patient context.
